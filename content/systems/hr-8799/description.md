# HR 8799: Four giant planets around a young, hot star

**HR 8799** is a young star with a temperature of **about 7400 K**, hotter than the Sun, and an age of **about 0.03 billion years**. Located roughly **135 light-years** from Earth, it hosts four Jupiter-like planets discovered by imaging: **HR 8799 b**, **HR 8799 c**, **HR 8799 d**, and **HR 8799 e**.

**HR 8799 b** carries a mass of **about 2000 Earth masses**, exceeding Earth's mass and equaling roughly **6.29 times Jupiter's mass**. Its radius spans **about 13 Earth radii**, larger than Earth's, while its mean density reaches **about 5.02 g/cm³**, about **0.91 times Earth's**. The planet's year lasts **about 170000 days**, far longer than Earth's roughly 365-day year. Among the four planets, **HR 8799 b** shows the smallest mass at **about 2000 Earth masses**.

**HR 8799 c** follows with a mass of **about 3000 Earth masses**, greater than Earth's and roughly **9.44 times Jupiter's mass**. Its radius also measures **about 13 Earth radii**, larger than Earth's, and its mean density comes to **about 7.52 g/cm³**, about **1.37 times Earth's**. This planet completes its year in **about 69000 days**, again exceeding Earth's roughly 365-day year.

**HR 8799 d** matches that mass at **about 3000 Earth masses**, likewise larger than Earth's and roughly **9.44 times Jupiter's mass**. Its radius holds at **about 13 Earth radii**, larger than Earth's, with a mean density of **about 7.52 g/cm³**, about **1.37 times Earth's**. Its year runs **about 37000 days**, longer than Earth's roughly 365-day year.

**HR 8799 e** stands out with the largest mass of the group at **about 3180 Earth masses**, exceeding Earth's and reaching roughly **10 times Jupiter's mass**. Its radius of **about 13.1 Earth radii** also tops the list, larger than Earth's. The mean density measures **about 7.76 g/cm³**, about **1.41 times Earth's**, and its year stretches beyond Earth's roughly 365-day year.
