# LHS 1140 and its two planets

**LHS 1140** is an **M4.5 V** star, a small, cool red dwarf. Its mass is **about 0.184 solar masses**, smaller than the Sun's, and its radius is **about 0.216 solar radii**, also smaller than the Sun's. Its temperature is **about 3100 K**, cooler than the Sun's. The star has a lower limit of **5 billion years** for its age, and it lies **about 48.9 light-years** away.

Two planets are associated with this host, both found by the transit method, in which a planet passes in front of its star from our viewpoint and causes a small dip in observed brightness. Repeated dips help establish orbital periods. **LHS 1140 b** was discovered in 2017, and **LHS 1140 c** in 2018.

**LHS 1140 b** is a Super-Earth. Its radius is **about 1.73 Earth radii**, larger than Earth's, and its planet mass, which is not a minimum-mass quantity, is **about 5.6 Earth masses**, larger than Earth's. Its mean density is **about 5.96 g/cm³**, **about 1.08 times Earth's**. Its year is **about 24.7 days**, shorter than Earth's roughly 365-day year, and among the listed planets it has the longest year.

**LHS 1140 c** is Earth-like. Its radius is **about 1.27 Earth radii**, larger than Earth's, and its planet mass, which is not a minimum-mass quantity, is **about 1.91 Earth masses**, larger than Earth's. Its mean density is **about 5.11 g/cm³**, **about 0.928 times Earth's**. Its year is **about 3.78 days**, shorter than Earth's roughly 365-day year, and among the listed planets it has the shortest year.

Among the listed planets, **LHS 1140 c** has the smallest radius, at **about 1.27 Earth radii**, and the smallest mass, at **about 1.91 Earth masses**. **LHS 1140 b** has the largest radius, at **about 1.73 Earth radii**, and the largest mass, at **about 5.6 Earth masses**.
