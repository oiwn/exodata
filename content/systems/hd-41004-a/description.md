# HD 41004 A and Its Planet

HD 41004 A is a K-type dwarf star—smaller and cooler than our Sun—located roughly 120 light-years away. The star's reported radius is about 0.85 solar radii, smaller than the Sun's, and its temperature is estimated at around 5310 K, cooler than the Sun's. HD 41004 A has an estimated mass of about 0.95 solar masses and a reported age upper limit of 1 billion years.

The star hosts one known planet, HD 41004 A b, discovered in 2004. This planet has a minimum-mass quantity (M sin i) of about 807 Earth masses, which is larger than Earth's mass and roughly 2.54 times Jupiter's mass. Since the orbit's tilt is unknown, this value represents a lower bound on the planet's true mass: the measurable gravitational pull gives at least this amount. HD 41004 A b completes its year in approximately 963 days, longer than Earth's roughly 365-day year.

The planet's year is the time it takes to complete one orbit around its host star, and the mass quantity reflects the gravitational influence it exerts on the star as detected through radial velocity measurements.
