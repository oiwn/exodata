# HD 10180 and its planets

HD 10180 is a Sun-like G-type star estimated to be about 127 light-years away. The star's reported mass is about 1.06 solar masses, making it larger than the Sun, and its reported temperature of about 5910 K is hotter than the Sun's. HD 10180 has an estimated age of about 4.3 billion years.

Six planets were detected around this star in 2010 using the radial velocity method. For each planet, the measured quantity is a minimum-mass value (M sin i), which represents a lower bound on the planet's true mass when the orbit's tilt is unknown.

HD 10180 c has the shortest year among the listed planets, completing an orbit in about 5.76 days, with a minimum-mass quantity of about 13.2 Earth masses—the second-smallest reported value in this system and larger than Earth's mass. HD 10180 d completes its orbit in about 16.4 days, the second-shortest year among these planets, and has the smallest reported minimum-mass quantity at about 12 Earth masses, still larger than Earth's mass.

HD 10180 e has a year of about 49.7 days and the second-largest reported minimum-mass quantity among the listed planets at about 25.6 Earth masses. HD 10180 f orbits with a year of about 123 days and has a minimum-mass quantity of about 22.9 Earth masses. HD 10180 g has the second-longest year by reported estimates, about 605 days, with a minimum-mass quantity of about 23.3 Earth masses.

HD 10180 h has the longest year among the listed planets at about 2210 days, with the largest reported minimum-mass quantity at about 65.7 Earth masses. All six planets have minimum-mass quantities larger than Earth's mass; the five planets with shorter years than Earth's roughly 365-day year are HD 10180 c, d, e, and f, while HD 10180 g and h have years longer than Earth's.
