# Kepler-16: a circumbinary world

**Kepler-16** is a binary star system about **245 light-years** away. Its selected host is a cool, low-mass star with a mass of about **0.63 solar masses**, a radius of about **0.769 solar radii**, and a temperature of about **4020 K**, all smaller or cooler than the Sun. The system contains two cataloged stars and one known planet.

That planet, **Kepler-16 b**, is a circumbinary world, meaning it orbits both stars rather than one alone. It was found by transit, the small dip in brightness when a planet crosses in front of its star. Its year, or orbital period, is about **229 days**, shorter than Earth's roughly **365-day year**.

**Kepler-16 b** has a radius of about **8.45 Earth radii**, larger than Earth's, and a mass of about **106 Earth masses**, also larger than Earth's. For its size, its mean density is about **0.967 g/cm³**, or about **0.175 times Earth's**, while **4 times Earth** would be the rough threshold for a Jupiter-like radius.
