# 55 Cnc and its planets

About 41 light-years away, the star 55 Cnc is a G-type dwarf, similar in character to our Sun but slightly smaller: its reported mass is about 0.96 solar masses, its radius about 0.93 solar radii, and its temperature roughly 5240 K—cooler than the Sun. The star is also estimated to be about 9.5 billion years old. Five planets are associated with this host, all discovered through the radial velocity method between 1996 and 2007.

The innermost system of planets includes 55 Cnc e, which stands out for its extreme year: at about 0.737 days, it has the shortest orbital period of the listed planets. This world carries a reported mass estimate of about 7.99 Earth masses (not a minimum-mass quantity) and a radius estimate of about 1.88 Earth radii—both larger than Earth's corresponding values. Its year is far shorter than Earth's roughly 365-day year.

Next in line by year length, 55 Cnc b completes an orbit in about 14.7 days, making it the planet with the second-shortest year among those listed. Its reported minimum-mass quantity (M sin i) is about 276 Earth masses—a lower bound on its true mass, since the orbital tilt is unknown—and is larger than Earth's mass.

55 Cnc c has a year of about 44.4 days, also shorter than Earth's year. Its minimum-mass quantity is about 56.6 Earth masses, again larger than Earth's mass. 55 Cnc f takes about 261 days to orbit once, giving it the second-longest year of the listed planets, though still shorter than an Earth year. Its minimum-mass quantity is roughly 48.5 Earth masses.

The most massive of the set is 55 Cnc d, whose reported minimum-mass quantity is about 1210 Earth masses—roughly 3.82 times Jupiter's mass—making it both more massive than Earth and far heavier than Jupiter. With an orbital period of about 4800 days, it holds the longest year among the associated planets, considerably longer than Earth's roughly 365-day year.
